::: {custom-style="Abstract" lang=en}
**Abstract:** Import tariff policy and non-tariff measures shape the market structure of riding apparel in Indonesia, so that every foreign brand faces different entry costs and quality requirements. Alpinestars is an Italian riding apparel brand competing in the premium segment of the national market. Competition in the upper segment requires an understanding of *brand equity*, *country of origin* perception, and the structure of import costs. This study aims to analyse how Indonesian import tariff and tax policy as well as non-tariff measures shape the market position of Alpinestars and the purchase outcomes of Indonesian consumers. This study employs a descriptive qualitative approach with document and secondary-source analysis, read through an integrated import policy, brand position, and consumer decision lens. The findings indicate that import duties, import value added tax, import income tax article 22, mandatory standards, and import prohibitions and restrictions raise selling prices while strengthening the premium brand position in the upper segment. Such price increases shift consumer considerations between quality and budget, so that the premium brand holds the upper segment while some consumers move to substitute products. In conclusion, import policy and consumer perception jointly determine the market position of Alpinestars and the purchase outcomes of Indonesian consumers in the riding apparel category.

**Keywords:** Alpinestars, import tariff, SNI, brand equity, country of origin

# Introduction <!-- kamus:h id=pendahuluan -->

Trade in riding apparel is an important channel for the expansion of the global manufacturing industry because demand grows along with rising motorcycle use in many countries [@nizar2015pola]. Emerging markets, including Indonesia, offer large demand volume with layered consumer structures. Import tariff policy and non-tariff measures, including Indonesian National Standard (SNI) requirements, shape how foreign brands enter and compete in the national market. Indonesia's tariff profile shows that the duty burden differs by tariff line, so that certain product groups are more protected than others [@anon2021indonesia]. In addition, the cost of complying with non-tariff measures in Indonesia is substantial for businesses, especially in the food and agriculture sector [@amanta2021cost]. The classification of non-tariff measures also shows that import prohibitions, restrictions, and licensing add layers of barriers beyond tariffs [@anon2019non]. Measuring the impact of such measures requires quantification methods that can translate non-tariff barriers into tariff equivalents [@bora2005quantification]. With this in mind, Indonesian import policy is not singular, but a combination of tariffs, taxes, and administrative requirements that determine prices and market access.

Studies on non-tariff measures show that such barriers affect product quality and import demand in destination countries [@yue2021non]. The calculation of tariff equivalents for core and non-core non-tariff measures is used to estimate the scale of hidden protection in the Asia Pacific region [@ando2005estimating]. Other studies affirm that non-tariff measures affect import competition and the export performance of developing countries [@cali2021non]. Non-tariff barriers have also long been faced by developing-country exports and tend to burden them more than tariffs [@sampson1989non]. A review of essays on international trade and non-tariff measures shows that such barriers are related to the quality of state institutions [@herghelegiunessays]. Moreover, tariff escalation in developing countries shows that protection rises with the degree of product processing [@laird1987empirical]. Import liberalisation in a country with import-intensive industry shows different effects depending on the local industrial structure [@khan1994import]. A study on global commodity price volatility affirms that developing-country import risk is related to world price fluctuations [@sarris2011global]. With this in mind, tariff policy and non-tariff measures are important determinants of the position of imported products in the domestic market.

In the realm of tariff policy, tariff incidence shows that the protection burden can shift between importers and consumers [@mitchener2026price]. A study on tariff liberalisation for wood products shows that tariff changes alter the price and consumption structure in a developing country [@soltanizadeh2024economic]. The balance between price fairness and industrial protection is also discussed in a review of import tariff policy on domestic consumer goods [@janati2025balance]. Price and tariff policy is proven to affect import volume for certain commodities in Indonesia [@antriyandarti2021effect]. In addition, Indonesia's non-tariff policy toward exports to Europe shows that non-tariff barriers matter more than tariffs [@gunawiredja2022pengaruh]. Trade facilitation and the strengthening of business readiness are directed at overcoming such barriers [@heriamsal2024meningkatkan]. Business readiness in facing a broader economic cooperation framework is also a determining factor [@ardelia2026kesiapan]. A competitiveness-strengthening programme is directed at overcoming non-tariff barriers through market diversification [@sipahutar2026program]. With this in mind, the trade-policy literature provides a basis for reading the influence of import policy on prices and consumer decisions.

On the consumer side, *brand equity* places brand awareness, brand association, perceived quality, and brand loyalty as the main dimensions of brand value [@aaker1996measuring]. Consumer-based brand equity measurement for brands in developing countries shows that these dimensions are interrelated [@lee2011dimensions]. A cross-country study affirms that brand equity measurement needs to consider the market context in which a brand is marketed [@christodoulides2015consumer]. A review of *country of origin* research shows that perception of product origin affects quality assessment and willingness to pay a higher price [@verlegh1999review]. In the helmet category, brand image and product quality affect purchase decisions [@zuniyanto2025brand]. Price is also a consideration that affects helmet purchase decisions [@darmawan2025pengaruh]. Factors influencing consumers to buy *full face* helmets include quality, price, and brand image [@bayuaji2010analisis]. The intention to use a helmet with a quality standard label is influenced by quality signals and brand awareness [@rakhmawati2020intention]. Product innovation is related to the competitiveness of the helmet industry when quality standards are enforced [@hartono2019influence]. With this in mind, the consumer literature provides a basis for reading the position of premium brands.

Riding apparel has a particular character because it functions as body protection and is subject to safety standards. A study on protective motorcycle apparel shows that the outer attributes of a product determine user assessment [@wishart2025when]. The standard for protective garments for motorcycle riders sets requirements and test methods that producers must meet [@anonnprotective] [@anonnprotectiveb]. The requirements for protective footwear for motorcycle riders are also set through a separate standard [@anonnprotectivea]. A study on helmets shows that construction and materials determine the ability to absorb impact energy [@obst2023motorbike]. Numerical simulation of helmet impact tests is used to assess protection performance [@aiello2007numerical]. Impact-absorbing materials also determine the effectiveness of protection [@desousa2012assessing]. Uncertainty in homologation testing according to safety standards shows that standard compliance demands strict quality control [@pratellesi2011effect]. In the textile and apparel industry, market and supply conditions determine the technology used by producers [@boon1981apparel]. The development of the adaptive apparel market shows that product innovation can open a new segment [@mcbeeblacknadaptive]. Protective apparel is also linked to the sustainability aspect of materials [@anon2015sustainable]. The selection of protective apparel in health care shows the importance of product suitability to user needs [@anon2005selection]. With this in mind, riding apparel sits between the demand for quality and the demand for price.

Alpinestars is a riding apparel brand from Italy that places quality and safety at the core of its product identity. The brand is known through its involvement in racing and the development of high-performance racing apparel. The development of racing boots shows that product engineering is carried out with riders to meet safety demands. Partnership with a racing team in the Asian market, including India, shows a brand expansion strategy through the competition channel as well as the formation of a quality reputation through racing product development [@timesofindia2023alpinestars]. The brand's presence at a racing event in Indonesia, such as the MXGP in Lombok, shows the brand's connection to the national market [@racerx2024jorgeprado]. The brand's position in the premium segment is related to brand equity and consumer perception of product origin. Nevertheless, studies linking Indonesian import policy to the market position of premium riding apparel brands remain rare. This research gap lies in the absence of an analysis that integrates a trade-policy lens and a consumer lens in an integrated manner.

To fill this gap, this study applies an integrated import policy, brand position, and consumer decision lens that combines an international political economy framework and a brand equity framework. The lens is used to read how import tariff and tax policy, non-tariff measures in the form of SNI, and import prohibitions and restrictions relate to the position of the Alpinestars brand and the decisions of Indonesian consumers. # Methods <!-- kamus:h id=metode -->

This study employs a descriptive qualitative approach with a document-analysis design on Indonesian import policy and the riding apparel market. The object of study is the Alpinestars brand as a premium riding apparel product from Italy, while the market scope covers import channels and the Indonesian market. The review period is limited to 2015–2025 so that tariff policy dynamics, non-tariff policy changes, and shifts in consumer preference can be traced in an up-to-date manner. The focus of analysis is directed at the relationship between import policy instruments, brand position, and consumer decisions. With this in mind, this design is chosen because the research question is explanatory of policy and market relations, rather than a statistical measurement of magnitude.

Data are collected through a search of official documents, industry reports, news, articles, and webpages. The collection procedure is carried out through a five-channel subject sweep covering press, news, article, official, and general web channels. Each subject is swept until saturation, namely three consecutive rounds without new findings. In addition, an academic search is carried out through reference databases to obtain relevant sources. Furthermore, to maintain traceability, every work used as evidence passes through the fulltext ladder before being used to claim content. The ladder covers title, abstract, and full-content checking in sequence. With this in mind, claims about source content are only taken from works whose full text can be examined.

This study does not use primary data because primary data collection lies beyond the scope of the study. As a result, no consumer survey, importer interview, or field price measurement is carried out. The subject sweep also shows that Alpinestars-specific coverage in Indonesia is thin, so that sources specific to the brand are limited in number. Therefore, the analysis is supported by general sources on Indonesian import policy and relevant consumer behaviour. As an example, news on national tariff policy and articles on helmet purchase decisions are used to complement the reading of the brand case. With this in mind, the limitation of source coverage is acknowledged and taken into account in interpreting the findings.

Data analysis is carried out through a thematic reading across three axes, namely import policy, brand position, and consumer decision. The import policy axis is used to map import duties, import taxes, and non-tariff measures applicable to riding apparel products. The brand position axis is used to read brand equity and country-of-origin perception in the premium segment. The consumer decision axis is used to trace the relationship between price, quality attributes, and purchase choice. Findings from each axis are compared with prior studies to assess agreement and difference. With this in mind, every finding is grounded in more than one type of source and one layer of evidence.

Validity is maintained through source triangulation by comparing information from several types of sources before drawing conclusions. First, official documents and reports are used to trace tariff policy, import taxes, and standard requirements. Second, news and articles are used to trace price dynamics and consumer response. Third, academic literature is used to place the findings within an international political economy framework and a consumer framework. The thematic reading is carried out by marking statements related to the three analysis axes. As a result, the conclusions drawn are analytical and open to testing through further research.

## Tariff and Import Tax Policy on Riding Products in Indonesia

Import policy on riding apparel products in Indonesia is composed of several complementary instruments. The first instrument is the import duty set according to the tariff line of goods, so that the amount of the levy differs across product groups [@anon2021indonesia]. In addition, tariff incidence shows that the levy burden can shift between importers and consumers according to the market structure [@mitchener2026price]. The pattern of tariff escalation also shows that protection tends to rise with the degree of product processing [@laird1987empirical]. As a result, finished products such as riding apparel and footwear face a higher entry burden than raw materials. With this in mind, the import duty acts as the initial determinant of imported product costs.

Tariff changes also alter the price and consumption structure in certain product markets. A study on tariff liberalisation for wood products shows that tariff reduction lowers prices and raises consumption in a developing country [@soltanizadeh2024economic]. Conversely, the classification of non-tariff measures shows that import prohibitions and restrictions can add barriers beyond tariffs [@anon2019non]. Measuring the impact of such measures requires tariff-equivalent calculation so that the scale of protection can be compared [@ando2005estimating]. In addition, quantification methods are used to estimate the effect of non-tariff measures on trade [@bora2005quantification]. With this in mind, import duties and non-tariff measures cannot be read separately because both jointly determine the entry cost.

The second instrument is the value added tax on imports, which adds a levy burden after the import duty. The third instrument is the income tax article 22 on imports, which is collected at the time of import and adds to the administrative cost of the importer. The balance between price fairness and industrial protection shows that import tariff policy on consumer goods touches two interests at once [@janati2025balance]. In addition, news shows that the direction of Indonesian tariff policy moves dynamically along with trade negotiations with major partners [@bbc2025tarif]. The government also agreed to a tariff reduction within the framework of a trade agreement with the United States while affirming the protection of workers' interests [@setkab2025tarif]. On the other hand, news notes that the import tariff on certain products into Indonesia is relatively low, so that public attention is more directed at a few commodities [@cnbcindonesia2025tarif]. With this in mind, import taxes reinforce the influence of import duties on selling prices.

The fourth instrument is the mandatory standard in the form of SNI and safety standards that govern the quality of riding apparel. The standard for protective garments for motorcycle riders sets requirements and test methods that producers must meet [@anonnprotective]. Similar requirements also apply to protective footwear for motorcycle riders through a separate standard [@anonnprotectivea]. The provisions on protective garments for motorcycle riders are also affirmed in another standard reference [@anonnprotectiveb]. Compliance with such standards demands strict quality control, especially when homologation testing involves uncertainty [@pratellesi2011effect]. As a result, the mandatory standard screens out players that cannot meet quality requirements. With this in mind, SNI acts as both a filter and a quality marker for consumers.

The fifth instrument is the import prohibition and restriction that governs the entry channel for goods. The cost of complying with non-tariff measures in Indonesia is substantial for businesses, especially in the food and agriculture sector [@amanta2021cost]. Indonesia's non-tariff barriers toward exports to Europe also show that non-tariff barriers matter more than tariffs [@gunawiredja2022pengaruh]. Non-tariff barriers have long been faced by developing-country exports and tend to burden them more than tariffs [@sampson1989non]. Non-tariff measures also affect import competition and the export performance of developing countries [@cali2021non]. In addition, such barriers affect product quality and import demand in destination countries [@yue2021non]. Those barriers are also related to the institutional quality of the exporting country [@herghelegiunessays]. With this in mind, import prohibitions and restrictions limit channel access and demand administrative readiness from businesses.

## The Position of Alpinestars in the Indonesian Riding Apparel Market

These four dimensions are interrelated in shaping consumer preference in developing-country markets [@lee2011dimensions]. Consumer-based brand equity measurement also needs to consider the market context in which a brand is marketed [@christodoulides2015consumer]. With this in mind, the strength of the Alpinestars brand rests on a combination of quality reputation and racing experience.

On the perceived quality dimension, riding apparel is assessed through its ability to protect the user. Construction and materials determine the ability of a product to absorb impact energy [@obst2023motorbike]. Numerical simulation of impact tests is used to assess the protection performance of helmets and related apparel [@aiello2007numerical]. Impact-absorbing materials also determine the effectiveness of protection [@desousa2012assessing]. On the brand association dimension, Italian origin is attached to the image of design and racing heritage. A review of country-of-origin research shows that perception of product origin affects quality assessment and willingness to pay a higher price [@verlegh1999review]. In the textile and apparel industry, market and supply conditions determine the technology used by producers [@boon1981apparel]. On the brand loyalty dimension, experienced rider consumers tend to make repeat purchases. ## Discussion

The discussion is directed at answering the research question on how Indonesian import tariff and tax policy as well as non-tariff measures shape the market position of Alpinestars and the purchase outcomes of Indonesian consumers. The findings show that brand position does not rest on a single factor. On the contrary, the position is determined by the alignment between import policy instruments, brand equity, and country-of-origin perception. This alignment plays an important role as the core of the integrated import policy, brand position, and consumer decision lens used in this study.

The findings on import duty and import tax are in line with studies that place tariff incidence as a determinant of price and demand [@mitchener2026price]. The findings on price changes due to tariffs support studies on the impact of tariff liberalisation on the consumption structure [@soltanizadeh2024economic]. With this in mind, the findings of this study reinforce the view that tariffs and import taxes are the main determinants of imported product prices.

The findings on non-tariff barriers mattering more than tariffs are in line with studies on Indonesian exports to Europe [@gunawiredja2022pengaruh]. In addition, the findings on the influence of non-tariff measures on import competition support cross-country studies [@cali2021non]. With this in mind, non-tariff measures become the second determinant after tariffs in shaping the cost structure of imported products.

The findings on quality and demand reinforce studies on the relationship between non-tariff measures and imported product quality [@yue2021non]. The findings on tariff-equivalent calculation support studies on estimating hidden protection [@ando2005estimating]. The findings on quantifying the impact of non-tariff measures also support related methodological studies [@bora2005quantification]. In addition, the findings on institutional quality as a determinant of success in overcoming barriers are in line with the review of essays on trade and non-tariff measures [@herghelegiunessays]. The findings on tariff escalation for finished products support studies on protection rising with processing [@laird1987empirical]. With this in mind, the findings of this study reinforce the trade-policy framework in reading the cost structure of imported products.

# Conclusion <!-- kamus:h id=simpulan -->

Based on the results and discussion, it can be concluded that Indonesian import tariff and tax policy as well as non-tariff measures jointly shape the market position of Alpinestars and the purchase outcomes of Indonesian consumers. Import duty, import value added tax, and import income tax article 22 raise the entry cost so that the selling price of imported products rises [@mitchener2026price]. The mandatory standard in the form of SNI and import prohibitions and restrictions screen out businesses and demand quality compliance [@amanta2021cost]. The position of Alpinestars in the premium segment is supported by brand equity and country-of-origin perception that strengthen quality assessment [@aaker1996measuring] [@verlegh1999review]. With this in mind, import policy and consumer perception are two sides that determine brand position in the riding apparel category.

Overall, the findings show that the price increase caused by import policy does not always weaken a premium brand. On the contrary, the price increase can strengthen the premium brand position because consumers who prioritise safety still choose quality products [@rakhmawati2020intention]. On the other hand, consumers with limited budgets tend to weigh substitute products in the middle segment [@darmawan2025pengaruh]. With this in mind, the influence of import policy on consumer purchase outcomes differs across buyer groups.

This study has limitations because it is sourced from documents and secondary literature without primary data collection. As a result, some claims regarding the magnitude of selling prices and brand market share cannot be verified directly. In addition, Alpinestars-specific coverage in Indonesia is thin, so that the analysis rests on general sources on import policy and consumer behaviour. Therefore, the conclusions drawn are analytical and not a definite measurement.

For future researchers, it is recommended to collect primary data through interviews with importers, distributors, and riding apparel consumers in order to obtain a more complete picture. Further studies can also compare several premium brands from Europe and Asia to obtain a broader understanding of competition patterns in the upper segment. The use of quantitative data on import volume and value has the potential to complement the qualitative findings in subsequent studies. For businesses, strengthening product quality and affirming brand identity should be a primary concern in maintaining a competitive position. For the government, transparency in the calculation of import duties, import taxes, and standard requirements should be maintained so that import policy balances industrial protection and consumer interests.
